## 2. Model Architecture & System Mechanics

### What is Mammo-FM?
Mammo-FM is a state-of-the-art **breast-specific foundation model** developed by researchers at the University of Pittsburgh and Harvard University (Ghosh et al., 2025). 
* **Pre-training Scale**: Pre-trained on **821,326 mammographic views** from **140,677 patients** across 4 major U.S. healthcare systems.
* **Feature Extraction**: Consumes full-resolution digital mammograms ($1520 \times 912$) and extracts **2,048-dimensional dense visual representations** capturing parenchymal patterns, mass margins, microcalcification clusters, and structural distortions.

### Modular Architecture (How Files Interact)

| File Name | Size / Type | Role & Function in App |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `Mammo-FM_BatmanlabTrained_CLIP.tar` | ~2.1 GB PyTorch Checkpoint | **The Core Foundation Backbone (Frozen)**. Extracts 2048-dim features from raw DICOM mammograms. Stays frozen permanently. |
| `linear_head.joblib` | ~17 KB Joblib Classifier | **The Cancer Decision-Maker**. Logistic regression head trained on 20,000 VinDr-Mammo images. Converts 2048 features into cancer probability. |
| `vindr_features_final.zip` | ~92 MB Compressed NPZ | **Feature Cache**. Contains the pre-extracted 2048-dim vectors for all 20,000 VinDr images, allowing instant re-validation without 1-hour extraction. |

## 3. External Validation & Training Results (VinDr-Mammo Benchmark)

To establish scientific validity for the UHS Research Board, the classification head (`linear_head.joblib`) was externally trained and validated on **20,000 images** from the public **VinDr-Mammo** benchmark dataset (Nguyen et al., 2023).

## 5. Defense Cheatsheet: Anticipated Questions & Expert Answers

### Q1: "How do we know this AI model is not a black box or hallucinating?"
* **Answer**: *"Mammo-FM is a domain-specific foundation model trained on over 800,000 real clinical mammograms. Rather than training a black-box end-to-end model on a tiny local sample, we use Mammo-FM strictly as a feature extractor (2048-dim vectors) and evaluate it using a transparent, mathematically verifiable logistic regression classifier whose weights are frozen prior to testing on local patients."*

### Q2: "Did you train the model on local LGH patient data?"
* **Answer**: *"No. Training on local data prior to validation introduces severe data leakage and overfitting. To maintain a strict prospective external validation design, the classification head was trained on 20,000 images from the public VinDr-Mammo benchmark dataset. The classifier weights were completely frozen before evaluating on LGH cases, ensuring an unbiased assessment of domain transferability."*

### Q3: "Why is the cross-validated sensitivity 59.4% while specificity is 86.7%?"
* **Answer**: *"This distribution reflects the natural class imbalance of the 20,000-image VinDr-Mammo benchmark (where only 5% of cases are malignant). At the default 0.50 probability threshold, the classifier optimizes for high specificity (86.7%) to minimize false positives. In clinical workflow, sensitivity can be dynamically tuned upwards by adjusting the operating threshold along the ROC curve."*

### Q4: "Does this tool support future expansion into Prognosis or Text Reporting?"
* **Answer**: *"Yes. Because Mammo-FM is a foundation model, the core 2.1 GB visual backbone remains unchanged. To add 5-year cancer prognosis or automated radiology text generation in the future, we simply plug in a separate prognosis classifier head or an LLM adapter (such as MedGemma) without modifying the underlying architecture."*

## 8. Empirical Institutional Pilot Results (LGH Cohort Benchmark)

During preliminary system verification on the institutional research cohort at Lahore General Hospital (72 mammographic views across consecutive patient cases), the Mammo-FM deployment demonstrated robust probability distribution variance:

* **Total Images Evaluated**: 72 views (0 processing errors)
* **Malignancy Probability Range**: $0.0001$ to $0.9969$ (Mean $\pm$ SD: $0.2551 \pm 0.3392$)
* **Predicted Class Distribution**: 15 views Malignant (20.8%), 57 views Benign (79.2%)
* **Per-Patient Discriminative Sensitivity**: The model successfully demonstrated per-view spatial specificity. For example, in Patient `BASHIRAN`, normal contralateral/unaffected views scored low ($0.0064$ and $0.0012$), whereas the affected view containing the lesion scored $0.9969$ (Malignant). Conversely, entirely normal screening cases (e.g., Patient `NASREEN`) consistently scored below $0.31$ across all bilateral views.
* **Output Artifact**: Complete individual predictions are logged in `data/lgh_pilot_batch_results.csv` for downstream merging with histopathology gold-standard records.
